01 — THE PROBLEM

A knowledge machine that doesn't understand knowledge

Hallucinations come from next-word sampling: the model produces plausible text with no fact-checker. Plausibility is not truth. If it cannot separate a justified true belief from a lucky guess from a confident fabrication, I have to build the part that does.

Epistemology already worked out that scaffolding. I just have to wire it in.

02 — THE CLASSIC RECIPE

Justified True Belief, and the Gettier problem in production

Nagel's classical analysis: you know something if you believe it, it is true, and you have justification. Flip the three switches.

switch 01
Belief
The model asserts it
switch 02
Truth
It matches the world
switch 03
Justification
Grounded in a real source
Flip all three to reach knowledge

The Gettier twist: you can have all three and still not have knowledge if the justification reached the truth by luck. A RAG system can retrieve a doc, generate a true answer, and cite a source, yet be right by accident because the chunk did not actually support the claim. RAG faithfulness evaluation is how I rule that out.

03 — MY ACTUAL THESIS

Fact vs knowledge vs opinion in unlabelled web data

When you ground a model on web data, you pour in three things wearing the same clothes. A tokeniser cannot tell them apart: a confident comment and a careful citation read the same.

TypeWhat it isOn the web it looks like
FactA true state of the world"Paris is the capital of France"
KnowledgeA justified, truth-tracking claimA peer-reviewed result with methodology
OpinionA belief with weak or no justification"Paris is the best city in the world"

To make a model decipher fact from knowledge from opinion in unlabelled data, I need an explicit theory of what separates them. That theory is epistemology. Without it I am hoping the model absorbs judgement by osmosis, which is how bias gets baked in. So I build the filter instead:

04 — TESTIMONY

Every retrieved chunk is testimony

Nagel devotes real space to testimony, how we know things because someone told us, and reductionism: do you trust testimony by default or demand independent backing? That is the RAG problem. A vector database is a testimony engine.

Reductionism says do not trust a source just because it spoke. That is source-quality scoring, reranking, and grounding only in verified databases. I was already building reductionist testimony filters. I just didn't have the word for it.

05 — CONTEXTUALISM

Trust thresholds should shift with the stakes

I landed on contextualism: the word "know" shifts weight with the situation. In Nagel's Lee case, Lee's claim to know the door is locked is fine when a coworker asks, but the bar jumps when four police officers ask. Same fact, different standard. 06 — SCEPTICISM

The healthy default

Nagel's scepticism chapter ("can we know anything at all?") trains a habit every ML engineer needs: never assume your model knows what it appears to know. Evaluation, red-teaming, and marking output unverified are all applied scepticism. Annoying at parties, essential in production.

07 — THE PAYOFF

How I leverage this to be a better engineer

01

Hallucination, with a why

Grounding and verification become a coherent strategy, not a checklist, because I know why plausibility diverges from truth.

02

Better RAG design

Retrieval as testimony plus faithfulness checks, not vibes. Source weighting and reranking with a reason behind them.

03

Context-aware trust

Contextualism justifies per-use-case grounding strictness: loose, cite-or-refuse, or human-in-the-loop.

04

Cleaner data judgement

A fact / knowledge / opinion distinction lets me reason about label quality instead of trusting every string equally.

05

Responsible-AI fluency

Veracity, transparency and explainability stop being AWS buzzwords and become epistemology I recognise.
